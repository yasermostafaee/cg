## Part 2 — the design (`openspec/changes/timeline-drives-loop-and-media/`)

D-133 and D-135 authored as ONE change, because the recon showed they are one question asked twice
— **what does the playhead drive, and what follows it** — landing on the same two functions and the
same protocol message. Marked DESIGN-FIRST; `tasks.md` §0 is the only complete section and every
implementation task carries an explicit `⟨GATE: §9.x⟩`.

### The headline decisions

**D-133 — the loop range maps onto the SHIPPED lifecycle, exactly. No new mode is proposed.**
`[contentStart → outPoint]` **is** the loop range, and looping is a **rendering of the existing
HOLD** rather than a new phase: today a content-driven hold parks the timeline on the single
`outPoint` frame while the drivers run; looping replaces that frozen frame with a repeating render
of the same interval. Start condition, end condition and the OUT phase are all unchanged.

🔴 **The seam rule you were most worried about is FREE.** The content drivers were never part of the
held range — a ticker's crawl position is not a function of the composition frame — so a wrap that
repaints the furniture **cannot** reset it. There is nothing connecting them. The mapping was chosen
precisely because it makes the restart _unreachable_ rather than merely forbidden. The invariant is
written as a test in `tasks.md` §2, not as a comment.

**D-135 — position by `currentTime`. Not play-and-re-anchor.** And it is not a preference: the
Designer transport **plays backward (`J`) and bounces**, and a `<video>` can do neither —
`playbackRate` must be positive. Play-and-re-anchor has **no behaviour at all** for two of the three
shipped transport modes. Two further supports: scrub and play are already the same call, so giving
play its own mechanism would fork the one function that guarantees they agree; and
`holdBehavior: 'freeze'` is the shipped proof that a positioned, paused `<video>` is a state this
engine already supports.

**The cost is stated rather than hidden** (`design.md` §5.2–5.3): per-frame seeking is the exact
thing `video-driver.ts` was hardened against, so a tick that finds a seek in flight must **skip**
rather than queue. **Canvas video will not be frame-accurate during playback and will drop frames
under decoder pressure.** The Preview keeps the real driver path and remains the frame-true
rendition. That contract is in the spec so it is not later filed as a bug.

## Corrections to the brief's recon starting points

You asked me to verify rather than trust. Two came back different:

- **"The driving-element set is computed in SIX places."** They still agree, but they are not six
  independent derivations: there is **one canonical predicate** (`hasEffectiveHoldDrivers`), **one
  documented mirror** of it in the runtime, **three call sites**, and **two predicates that are not
  the driving set at all**. One of those two — `hasContentElement`, which gates "Pin content start"
  — is **narrower** than the hold rule: it counts ticker/sequence/countdown clock but **not** an
  opted-in Lottie or video. So a composition held by a Lottie has a content-driven hold today and no
  way to pin its content start. D-133 removing that gate fixes it as a side effect. Nothing here
  needs consolidating; what needs care is that D-133 must not add a seventh site.
- **The framing that D-135 has two halves needing reconciliation.** On the canvas they are
  **already one mechanism** — the transport only calls `setCurrentFrame`, which posts
  `{ action: 'scrub', frame }`, which calls `runtime.tick(frame)`. The canvas **never receives the
  `play` action at all**; that path belongs to the Preview modal. D-135's whole gap is that
  `tick(frame)` reaches keyframed properties, repeater rows and lifespan gates — and **no Lottie
  player and no `<video>`**. One function.

Confirmed as given: `video-driver.ts`'s architecture statement verbatim; no stepped or deterministic
frame driver anywhere in the engine; `holdBehavior: 'freeze'` as the positioned-`<video>` precedent;
and SESSION I's `live()` resolver, whose lesson this design inherits as a hard constraint — the
canvas is a **second host that reparents nodes**, so per-frame positioning must go through `live()`
and never a captured reference, or B-137 reappears one surface over.
