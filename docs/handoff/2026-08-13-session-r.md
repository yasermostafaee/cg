## 1. The defect — fixed

With an infinite ticker and a Lottie under a content-driven hold, ticking the Lottie in "Which
content closes the graphic?" made the _"This graphic won't auto-close"_ caution **disappear** —
while the graphic still would not auto-close.

The condition escalated on **EVERY** effective driver being infinite. Ticking the Lottie added a
FINITE driver (a `freeze` hold completes at its intro end), the equality broke, and the banner
unmounted. Its headline claim was still true; only the subordinate clause ("every content driver
repeats forever") had stopped applying, and the code hid the whole assertion rather than rewording
it.

It now escalates on **ANY** effective infinite driver — own or nested, per-instance overrides
applied — which is the same set the runtime's `Promise.all` waits on. The function's own comment
had said ANY for as long as the code had said EVERY.

**One message, parameterised, rather than a separate all-infinite variant.** You asked me to decide
and say why: the REMEDY is identical in both cases, and naming the culprits is strictly more useful
than asserting "every" — which is the same list spelled less helpfully, and which is exactly the
wording that went stale the moment a finite driver joined. So the banner now reads
_"…won't auto-close — "Crawl" repeats forever, so the content-driven hold runs until stop"_, with
the remedy line unchanged. A driver reachable only through a nested instance is named by its
INSTANCE, because a deeper driver has no row of its own to point at.

## 2. 🔴 A second defect, found while building the fixture — also fixed

`mediaHoldItem` computed `infinite: el.holdBehavior === 'loop'`. **The two media kinds spell it
differently:** a video's never-completing hold is `loop`, a Lottie's is `idle-loop` (schema enums
`['loop','freeze']` and `['freeze','idle-loop']`). So **every idle-loop Lottie was marked a finite
closer** — no infinity chip on its row, and no escalation — while the runtime says the opposite in
as many words: _"an idle-loop Lottie holds until stop(), like an infinite ticker"_, and only a
freeze Lottie ever resolves `whenComplete`.

It was one line, in the same function feeding the same banner, and leaving it would have meant
shipping a fix to an alert that still lied in the neighbouring case. Not in your brief; flagged
here because it was found rather than sought.

## 3. The pattern you asked me to name

**This banner has now been made inaccurate twice by a change that looked local:**

1. **In styling (#352).** Recolouring it danger→caution changed the variant, and the
   variant-derived ARIA role silently demoted it from `alert` to `status` — an assertive
   announcement quietly became a polite one. The fix was to state the role explicitly, and the
   comment recording that is still there.
2. **In logic (this session).** Extending the checklist to media added finite drivers to a scene
   that previously had only infinite ones, and the EVERY condition — correct while media could not
   be listed — became wrong without anyone touching it.

What the two share is more useful than either: **both times the banner kept rendering something, so
nothing looked broken.** A control that goes silent is visible; a control that goes _slightly_ wrong
is not. The counter-measure now in the code is to keep the variant and the role BOTH explicit, and
to tie the condition to the CONSEQUENCE (`Promise.all` semantics) rather than to a special case of
the cause — and the tests assert `role="alert"` directly, in the mixed case, because that is the
property that has already regressed once.

A third instance is worth watching for: this is the same shape as the D-128 read/write split fixed
in session Q, where the closer LIST was extended and the mutator behind it was not.
